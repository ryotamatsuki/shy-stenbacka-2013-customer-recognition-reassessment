\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

We retain the primitives and timing of the inspected model and normalize only where doing so simplifies the price analysis.

\subsection{Consumers and switching}

There are two firms, \(A\) and \(B\), and four valuation types \(t\in\{HH,HL,LH,LL\}\). Let
\[
\Delta=v_H-v_L>0.
\]
For a consumer inherited by firm \(A\), define \(d_t=v_A^t-v_B^t\). The four offsets are
\[
d_{HH}=d_{LL}=0,\qquad d_{HL}=\Delta,\qquad d_{LH}=-\Delta.
\]
A consumer who switches pays \(\sigma s\), where \(s\sim U[0,1]\) in the baseline model and \(\sigma>0\). If the incumbent charges \(p\) and the rival's poaching price is \(q\), an \(A\)-inherited type-\(t\) consumer switches when
\[
v_B^t-q-\sigma s>v_A^t-p.
\]
Thus the switching mass is
\[
s_t(p,q)=
\left[\frac{p-q-d_t}{\sigma}\right]_0^1,
\qquad
[x]_0^1\equiv\min\{1,\max\{0,x\}\}.
\]
The clipping operator is part of the global demand correspondence; interior threshold formulas alone are not sufficient when a price deviation can cause a group to enter or leave demand. The analysis for consumers inherited by \(B\) is symmetric.

The maintained parameter restriction in the source is
\[
\sigma>3\Delta,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
0<z\equiv\Delta/\sigma<1/3.
\]

\subsection{Timing and information}

The game has three stages.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Each firm chooses whether to share customer information, \(S\) or \(NS\).
\item Each firm chooses whether to invest in customer recognition, \(I\) or \(NI\), paying acquisition cost \(c\) if it invests.
\item Firms choose incumbent and poaching prices subject to the information available after Stages I and II.
\end{enumerate}

It is useful to strip the application labels from the terminal price controls. For one inherited customer base, three control patterns appear in the source histories:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(TT\): both incumbent and poaching prices are type specific;
\item \(UU\): both are uniform across the four valuation types;
\item \(TU\): the incumbent price is type specific while the poaching price is uniform.
\end{itemize}
The source price profiles decompose as follows: full information uses \(TT+TT\); no information uses \(UU+UU\); one investor with sharing uses \(TT+UU\); both investors without sharing use \(TU+TU\); one investor without sharing uses \(TU+UU\); and the asymmetric sharing history with both firms investing uses \(TT+TU\).

This decomposition isolates the issue. The global-equilibrium failure arises in \(UU\), where a common price couples customer groups. The \(TT\) and \(TU\) components are separately re-audited and remain globally valid over the maintained domain.

\subsection{Normalization of the affected component}

Set \(\sigma=1\) temporarily and write \(x=p-q\). Summing the four switching masses in a \(UU\) inherited base gives
\[
F_z(x)=2[x]_0^1+[x+z]_0^1+[x-z]_0^1.
\]
The incumbent and poacher profits from this base are
\begin{equation}
u_I(p,q)=p[4-F_z(p-q)],
\qquad
u_P(q,p)=qF_z(p-q).
\label{eq:segmentpayoffs}
\end{equation}
The full no-information four-control game separates into two independent copies, \((p_A,q_B)\) and \((p_B,q_A)\). It is therefore sufficient to characterize the segment game globally.

When we return to Stages I and II we normalize acquisition cost as
\[
k=c/\sigma.
\]
All reported thresholds can then be rescaled by \(\sigma\) if desired.
