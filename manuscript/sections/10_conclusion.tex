\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper re-solves the customer-recognition price game in the complete working-paper predecessor of Shy and Stenbacka's 2013 article.  The key correction is global: a common poaching price that is locally optimal while all customer groups switch can be dominated by a finite deviation that abandons one mismatch group.  The source pure profile remains valid only up to
\[
z_c=\frac{-6+4\sqrt3}{3}.
\]
Above that threshold, but still inside the maintained \(z<1/3\) domain, the baseline no-information price game has no pure equilibrium.

A certified symmetric two-point mixed continuation permits the backward induction to be completed without imposing a new primitive or an ad hoc refinement.  Under that continuation the recognition-investment thresholds, Stage-I sharing correspondence, profit ranking, and consumer-surplus ranking require correction.  The total-welfare ordering survives.  These results show why a terminal price-game audit should extend beyond first-order conditions when common controls aggregate heterogeneous segments.

The paper's boundary is as important as its correction.  We do not establish uniqueness of the mixed continuation, broad portability to alternative switching-cost or type-mass structures, or a generic theorem about customer recognition.  Two pre-specified portability tests instead support a model-specific classification.  Targeted formal verification checks the proof-critical algebra but does not substitute for the economic active-set and equilibrium audits.

All equation-specific source claims refer to the directly inspected February 2012 working-paper model.  A direct equation-level comparison with the 2013 version of record would clarify attribution, but it is not required for the internal mathematical conclusions established here.
