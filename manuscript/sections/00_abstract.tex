\begin{abstract}
We reassess the pricing and information-acquisition logic of the customer-recognition model in the complete February 2012 working-paper version of Shy and Stenbacka, the verified predecessor of their 2013 \emph{Information Economics and Policy} article. The no-information pricing problem aggregates four customer groups under a common price and therefore has a kinked demand schedule. We show that the reported interior pure-price profile is globally valid only when
\[
\Delta/\sigma\le z_c\equiv(-6+4\sqrt{3})/3.
\]
For the admissible interval \(z_c<\Delta/\sigma<1/3\), the baseline price game has no pure-strategy equilibrium. We construct a symmetric two-point mixed continuation and use it to redo the model's backward induction. The correction changes the private thresholds for customer-recognition investment, the Stage-I sharing correspondence, and the rankings of gross profit and consumer surplus over part of the maintained parameter space. The broad total-welfare ordering nevertheless survives under the constructed continuation. Independent primitive-demand calculations, regime and boundary audits, and targeted Lean verification of the proof-critical algebra support the analysis. Two pre-specified portability tests overturn the high-mismatch pure-nonexistence result, so the contribution is explicitly model-specific rather than a general theorem about customer recognition or switching-cost competition. Because we have not directly inspected the equation-level version of record, all equation-specific correction claims refer to the verified working-paper model.
\end{abstract}
