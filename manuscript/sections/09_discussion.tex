\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Three aspects of the reassessment are worth emphasizing.

First, terminal-game globality matters disproportionately in sequential models.  The original all-groups-active price profile is locally well behaved: it satisfies the smooth first-order conditions and remains an incumbent best response throughout the source domain.  The problem arises only from a finite poaching deviation across a type-exit kink.  Once that deviation invalidates the no-information continuation, however, every earlier comparison that uses its payoff must be recomputed.  A local price-game issue therefore changes the private value of customer recognition and the information-sharing equilibrium correspondence.

Second, equilibrium correction and welfare correction need not move together.  The high-\(z\) price continuation materially changes profits and consumer surplus, yet the total-welfare ordering survives.  The reason is accounting as well as allocation: price changes redistribute surplus between firms and consumers, whereas total welfare responds to switching and information-acquisition costs.  A reassessment that stopped after identifying the profitable deviation would miss this separation.

Third, the negative portability result disciplines interpretation.  The exact pure-nonexistence interval is not robust to either a smooth non-uniform switching-cost distribution or a simple unequal-mass perturbation in our pre-specified diagnostics.  That sensitivity does not weaken the internal correction; it identifies which assumptions carry it.  The practical lesson is not that customer recognition generically creates mixed pricing, but that constrained common prices across heterogeneous segments should be audited globally whenever demand changes regime.

The selected high-\(z\) continuation is another deliberate boundary.  We prove that the displayed two-point profile is an equilibrium, not that it is the only mixed equilibrium.  A different mixed equilibrium with different continuation payoffs, if one exists, could change the high-\(z\) backward-induction and welfare numbers.  The pure correspondence, the exact counterexample, and the need to replace the pure continuation would remain.  This separation should make it straightforward for future work to strengthen the equilibrium-set result without conflating it with what is already established.

Finally, the source-version qualification should be read as an evidence rule rather than a substantive hedge.  The complete working-paper model is sufficient to support the mathematical reassessment presented here, and its publication lineage is verified.  We simply avoid assigning an equation-level defect to a version of record whose mathematical body we have not directly compared.
