## Candidate matrix

### 1. Information Economics and Policy — PRIMARY

**Current scope evidence.** Elsevier describes IEP as publishing peer-reviewed policy-oriented research on the production, distribution, and use of information, including the role of information in market functioning; it publishes research papers, short contributions, and surveys.

**Direct audience evidence.**
- the original 2013 article appeared in IEP;
- IEP published “Information sharing, personalized pricing, and collusion” in 2024;
- IEP published “Behavior-based algorithmic pricing” in 2024;
- IEP published “Personalized pricing with imperfect customer recognition” in 2025.

**Fit.**
- exact topical fit: customer information, recognition, sharing, pricing, welfare;
- source-journal continuity makes a source-specific reassessment editorially intelligible;
- downstream welfare/information consequences are more than an isolated algebraic correction;
- full-paper format is compatible with IEP’s research-paper category.

**Risks.**
- equation-level VOR/WP identity is not directly verified;
- contribution is model-specific after failed portability tests;
- editor may prefer a shorter correction if the downstream reassessment is viewed as too source-bound.

**Positioning response.**
The manuscript must lead with the verified predecessor-model scope, complete pure correspondence, selected mixed continuation, and downstream investment/CS/welfare consequences. It must not claim to correct a specific VOR equation.

**Stage-12 classification:** **PRIMARY / BEST FIT**.

### 2. Review of Industrial Organization — REALISTIC FALLBACK

Current publications include formal pricing papers such as “Random Pricing: Bertrand Competition with Uncontested Consumers” (2025), confirming an audience for mixed/random pricing and consumer segmentation.

Strengths:
- direct industrial-organization audience;
- model-specific pricing mechanisms can be publishable when the equilibrium result is sharp;
- less tied than IEP to whether the 2013 VOR equation is textually identical.

Risks:
- the paper is organized as a reassessment of another journal’s information-economics model;
- referee may ask for a broader mechanism or stronger mixed-equilibrium characterization.

**Classification:** **REALISTIC FALLBACK**.

### 3. Journal of Industry, Competition and Trade — SECOND FALLBACK

The journal publishes theoretical and empirical work on industrial organization, competition, market structure, and policy, and current 2026 issues include formal equilibrium papers.

Strengths:
- oligopoly/pricing/welfare content fits;
- model-specific theoretical contributions are not inherently outside scope.

Risks:
- customer-information angle is less central than at IEP;
- paper has limited direct trade/industrial-policy content.

**Classification:** **SECOND FALLBACK**.

### 4. Research in Economics — STRETCH / CONTRIBUTION-MISMATCH RISK

Elsevier describes RiE as a general-interest journal selecting original theoretical and empirical articles intended to have high impact on debate across the social sciences.

Strengths:
- theorem-heavy correction and reproducibility are methodologically compatible;
- explicit editorial interest in correctness/coherence.

Risks:
- a model-specific reassessment of one prior model may not clear a general-interest significance bar;
- Stage-7.5A portability failures should not be hidden to improve fit.

**Classification:** **STRETCH, NOT PRIMARY**.

### 5. International Journal of Industrial Organization — TOPIC FIT, STRENGTH MISMATCH

IJIO explicitly covers theoretical and empirical industrial-organization questions and classic strategic behavior/market structure.

Strength:
- subject matter is unquestionably IO.

Risk:
- the frozen contribution is explicitly model-specific and generic mixed-pricing/nonexistence mechanisms are known prior art;
- likely objection is scientific significance/generality rather than topical fit.

**Classification:** **DO NOT LEAD WITH IJIO**.

### 6. Economics Bulletin — SAFETY-NET NOTE ROUTE ONLY

Current submission portal states peer-reviewed Notes, Comments, and Preliminary Results must be seven printed pages or fewer excluding tables, figures, and references.

Strength:
- correction/comment format is explicit.

Risk:
- the certified full reassessment requires Stage-III, Stage-II, Stage-I, profit, CS, welfare, scope, and verification discussion;
- compressing to seven pages would require a separate note architecture and would discard much of the certified downstream contribution.

**Classification:** **SAFETY NET ONLY; would require a new compact-manuscript route, not the current Stage-10 paper.**

## Primary target decision

**PRIMARY TARGET: Information Economics and Policy.**

Reason:
the journal is simultaneously the source-paper venue, the strongest topical audience for customer recognition/information sharing, and an active current outlet for related personalized-pricing theory. The paper’s strongest contribution is not general mixed pricing but a source-specific equilibrium correction with information-acquisition and welfare consequences, which is most intelligible to this readership.

## Submission-risk posture

The cover letter must explicitly disclose:
1. the paper directly re-verifies the complete 2012 working-paper predecessor model;
2. bibliographic lineage to the 2013 IEP article is verified;
3. equation-level VOR identity was not directly inspected, so no VOR-specific equation-error claim is made;
4. the contribution is model-specific after two failed portability tests;
5. the paper nevertheless changes equilibrium existence and multiple downstream Results in the inspected model.

This source transparency is a strength of the submission package, not something to conceal.
