# Cover Letter — Information Economics and Policy

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript **“Customer Recognition and Information Exchange with Kinked Price Competition: A Reassessment of the Model behind Shy and Stenbacka (2013)”** for publication as a research paper in *Information Economics and Policy*.

The paper re-examines the complete February 2012 Federal Reserve Bank of Boston working-paper model that is the verified predecessor of Shy and Stenbacka’s 2013 *Information Economics and Policy* article. The analysis identifies a global-equilibrium issue in the no-information price subgame that is invisible to the local first-order conditions. In the inspected baseline model, the familiar pure price profile is the unique pure equilibrium only up to the exact mismatch threshold
[
z_c=(-6+4\sqrt{3})/3.
]
Above this threshold, but still within the maintained source domain, the baseline price game has no pure-strategy equilibrium. The manuscript constructs a certified symmetric two-point mixed continuation and uses it to redo the model’s backward induction.

The correction has substantive downstream consequences. It changes the private thresholds for customer-recognition investment, the Stage-I information-sharing correspondence, and the ranking of gross profit and consumer surplus over part of the maintained parameter space. At the same time, the broad total-welfare ordering survives under the constructed continuation. The manuscript therefore distinguishes equilibrium existence, private information incentives, consumer surplus, and total welfare rather than treating the issue as an isolated equation correction.

The contribution is intentionally scoped. Mixed pricing and pure-nonexistence mechanisms are established in related pricing literatures, so the manuscript does not present them as generic novelties. Two pre-specified portability tests also show that the strongest high-mismatch pure-nonexistence result is model-specific. The paper reports those negative robustness results explicitly. Likewise, the mixed continuation is certified as an equilibrium, but unrestricted mixed-equilibrium uniqueness is not claimed.

I also want to make the source boundary transparent. I directly verified the complete 2012 working-paper model and the bibliographic lineage to the 2013 journal article, but I have not directly inspected an equation-level copy of the version of record. Accordingly, the manuscript does not claim that a specific equation in the published 2013 article is erroneous; equation-specific statements are tied to the inspected predecessor model.

The manuscript includes reproducible Python/SymPy checks, independent primitive-demand and welfare evaluations, and a targeted Lean 4/mathlib verification of the proof-critical algebraic core. The formal-verification claims are explicitly limited to the encoded algebra and do not extend to the entire economic model.

*Information Economics and Policy* is the natural audience because the original model appeared in the journal and the reassessment concerns customer information, recognition, information sharing, pricing, and welfare—themes that remain active in the journal’s current scope.

This manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. This research received no external funding, and I declare no competing interests. The manuscript contains the required generative-AI disclosure and describes AI-assisted research/code support transparently.
