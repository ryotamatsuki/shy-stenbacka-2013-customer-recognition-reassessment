# Cover Letter — Information Economics and Policy

Dear Editor,

Please consider **“Customer Recognition and Information Exchange with Kinked Price Competition: A Reassessment of the Model behind Shy and Stenbacka (2013)”** as a research paper in *Information Economics and Policy*.

The manuscript re-examines the complete February 2012 Federal Reserve Bank of Boston working-paper model that is the verified predecessor of Shy and Stenbacka’s 2013 *Information Economics and Policy* article. It identifies a global-equilibrium issue in the no-information price subgame that is not detected by the local first-order conditions. In the inspected baseline model, the reported pure price profile is the unique pure equilibrium only up to the exact mismatch threshold z_c = (-6 + 4√3)/3. Above this threshold, while still inside the maintained source domain, the baseline price game has no pure-strategy equilibrium.

The paper constructs a certified symmetric two-point mixed continuation and redoes the model’s backward induction. The corrected continuation changes the private thresholds for customer-recognition investment, the Stage-I information-sharing correspondence, and the rankings of gross profit and consumer surplus over part of the maintained parameter space. The broad total-welfare ordering nevertheless survives.

The contribution is intentionally scoped. Mixed pricing and pure-nonexistence mechanisms are established in related pricing literatures, and the manuscript does not present them as generic novelties. Two pre-specified portability tests show that the strongest high-mismatch pure-nonexistence result is model-specific. The mixed continuation is certified as an equilibrium, but unrestricted mixed-equilibrium uniqueness is not claimed.

The source boundary is also explicit. I directly verified the complete 2012 working-paper model and its bibliographic lineage to the 2013 journal article, but I have not directly inspected an equation-level copy of the version of record. Accordingly, the manuscript does not claim that a specific equation in the published article is erroneous; equation-specific statements refer to the inspected predecessor model.

The submission includes reproducible Python/SymPy checks, independent primitive-demand and welfare evaluations, and targeted Lean 4/mathlib verification of the proof-critical algebraic core. Formal-verification claims do not extend to the complete economic model.

*Information Economics and Policy* is the natural audience because the reassessment concerns customer information, recognition, information sharing, pricing, and welfare, and because the source model appeared in the journal.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. This research received no external funding, and I declare no competing interests. The manuscript includes the required generative-AI disclosure and a separate description of AI-assisted research/code support.
